Physics Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion (EDI) encompasses frameworks and processes of improving representation and diversity in the field of physics. These aims go far beyond a diversity-as-numbers approach. Through EDI practices, we aim to reduce the structural and systemic harms of white supremacist, ableist, cis-heteropatriarchy and other worldviews which position white Eurocentric, heterosexual, gender normative, able-bodied, and male dominant values as superior to other forms of knowing and being in the world. Such worldviews are born of the colonialism, settler colonialism, ethnic cleansing, and genocide that Western European nations and institutions are founded upon. The effects of this violence reverberate and perpetuate in many forms, seen and unseen, today.

Systemic discrimination---in addition to individual bias---continues to make science inaccessible to historically excluded communities along a variety of lines of identity (race, skin colour, gender identity and expression, sexuality, dis/ability, neurodiversity, economic status, religion, immigration status, language, etc.). As a result, physics academia has been historically dominated by white, male, European individuals. Today, academia is plagued by a "leaky pipeline" whereby the participation of individuals with marginalised and underrepresented identities reduces at each step of the academic ladder due to systemic barriers. Taking explicit action to assuage these barriers and harms is imperative for creating a science founded in life-affirming humanism. In doing this work, we must always consider our positionalities and privileges as well as the intersectional dimensions of systemic harm: many historically excluded individuals suffer from "multiple jeopardy," the compounding effects of systemic barriers along multiple axes of identity. We must centre the voices of those who experience systemic barriers and harm in our institutions.

No resource list is comprehensive. However, I hope the content below provides a useful jumping-off point for those seeking resources and support as well as those hoping to learn more about furthering EDI in physics. I have tried to include some brief notes on each of the points, and I intend to fill out some major takeaways as I make my way through the material myself. It is important to recognise that tension, discomfort, and constant iteration are to be expected. In fact, frictional spaces---where we are constantly forced to unlearn, learn, and relearn many things we previously thought static---are the most generative and productive. Remember to give yourself time, space, and grace when engaging with this material!

	- Definitions of EDI: https://www.mcgill.ca/equity/resources/definitions
		- **Equity**: Equity, unlike the notion of equality, is not about sameness of treatment. Equity denotes fairness and justice in process and in results. Equitable outcomes often require differential treatment and resource redistribution so as to achieve a level playing field among all individuals and communities. This requires recognizing and addressing barriers to to provide opportunity for all individuals and communities to thrive in our University environment
		- **Diversity**: Diversity describes the presence of difference within any collection of people. In discussions of social equity, diversity addresses differences in social group membership related, for example, to race, Indigenous identity, class, gender identity or expression, sexuality, ability, ethnicity, and religion. Discussions about diversity linked to access and equity require knowledge and understanding of historical and contemporary experiences of oppression and exclusion. It’s important to think of diversity as uniting rather than dividing. Diversity means appreciating our differences but also our interconnectedness, recognizing systemic and institutionalized discrimination, and building relationships across our differences. That can be challenging, but it’s worth it. While appreciating social difference is important, a commitment to diversity should prompt us also to acknowledge all the things that connect us.
		- **Inclusion**: Inclusion refers to the notion of belonging, feeling welcome, having a sense of citizenship, and the capacity to engage and succeed in a given institution, program, or setting. Inclusion calls for recognizing, reducing, and removing barriers to participation and belonging, sometimes entailing the change or reimagination of such institutions, programs, or settings. Inclusion means welcoming and valuing all members of our University community.

- Healthy Brains Healthy Lives/Neuroscience EDI Initiatives: https://www.mcgill.ca/hbhl/edi. Aims: inclusion, advocacy, action, institutional change. Note: It will be useful to draw on the work/initiatives in other McGill departments to inform our own!
	- "In 2024, Healthy Brains, Healthy Lives (HBHL) conducted the third biennial online survey focused on Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI). The goal of the survey is to better understand the current environment at McGill by exploring the community's sense of inclusion and belonging, access to support systems and experiences with harassment and discrimination. It also aims to investigate how, and to what extent, these experiences may differ based on individuals' identity factors."
	- Key Findings
		- Inclusion sentiment: sense of inclusion tends to be lower among minoritised communities.
		- Access to support and opportunities: incomplete access to support/accommodations needed to fully participate. Disparities in access were associated with gender, race, age, disability, health, 2SLGBTQIA+ and first-generation university student status.
		- Harassment and discrimination: experiences were more common among members of several groups, including 2SLGBTQIA+ respondents, women faculty, trainees who are not Canadian citizens and individuals over the age of 45.
		- Growing sentiment that there is too much emphasis on EDI, primarily from individuals who do not identify within equity-deserving or minoritised groups.
	- Action Plan and Report: https://www.mcgill.ca/hbhl/edi/edi-action-plan-and-report
		- Areas of Focus
			- Governance: Ensuring meaningful inclusion of diverse voices in decision-making.
			- Recruitment and retention: Facilitating equitable recruitment and creating a positive climate to promote retention of faculty and staff.
			- Training: Providing accessible professional development for neuroscience trainees and promoting EDI best practices in research training environments.
			- Distribution of funding: Implementing equitable application and selection processes for HBHL funding programs.
			- Research content: Considering sex- and gender-based analysis plus (SGBA+) factors (e.g. sex, gender, age, ability, Indigeneity, geography, culture, income, sexual orientation, education, ethnicity, religion and language) in scientific research to inform equitable health care, technology and policy.
	- EDI in Funding Applications: https://www.mcgill.ca/hbhl/edi/edi-and-sgba-funding-applications. Note: Building EDI and ethics into performance and funding evaluations is a great way to implement institutional top-down change, but often requires grassroots, bottom-up pressure to implement. Top-down modes of enacting change are to be taken with care, as they can prompt ire. For instance, I'm inclined to echo Judith Butler in *Who's Afraid of Gender?* by saying that the International Monetary Fund (IMF) attempting to impose enforcement of rights as conditions on accessing loans may play into the anti-gender movement's falsified conception of 2SLGBTQ+ freedom as Western and colonial.
